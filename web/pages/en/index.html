The new Uzbek alphabet in one paragraph

Law {{lawNo}}, signed on {{signedDateEn}}, takes effect on {{lawDateEn}}. The alphabet now has 28 letters plus the tutuq sign (ʼ): O‘ → Ö, G‘ → Ğ, Sh → Ş, Ch → Ç, and Ng is no longer a separate letter. Documents, banknotes and securities issued in the old script stay valid.

A aB bD dE eF fG gĞ ğH hI iJ jK kL lM mN nO oÖ öP pQ qR rS sŞ şÇ çT tU uV vX xY yZ zʼ

School textbooks move over gradually: first-grade books from 2027 and the rest by 2031, according to the Ministry of Preschool and School Education.

What bitg does

  • Converts as you type in Word, Telegram, browsers and any other Windows app.
  • Leaves English alone: every word is checked against Uzbek and English dictionaries and the sentence context, so check, shop, links, e-mail addresses and code stay untouched. In our benchmark, 99.73% of English sentences came through unchanged.
  • One-key undo: press Backspace right after a replacement to get the original word back.
  • Reads Cyrillic: Cyrillic Uzbek goes straight to the new Latin script.
  • Works offline: nothing you type is sent anywhere or stored.
  • Optional layouts for typing the new letters directly: Tilmoch, Turkish Q, AltGr or your own.

Install in a minute

  1. Download the MSI for Windows 10/11 (x64, {{msiSizeMb}} MB) and open it.
  2. bitg adds itself to the “Uzbek (Latin)” language.
  3. Press Win + Space and pick bitg.

The beta installer is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may warn you: click “More info” → “Run anyway”. The SHA-256 checksum is on the download page.

Just need to convert some text?

Paste it into the online converter: old Latin or Cyrillic to the new alphabet, back to the old one, or to Cyrillic. It runs entirely in your browser.

Other platforms and developers

Linux: an IBus engine for Ubuntu 22.04+ and Debian 12+ is available as a .deb package ({{debSizeUz}}); a Fcitx5 add-on is in testing. iPhone, Android and Mac versions are planned. The conversion core is a dependency-free Rust library with a CLI, a C ABI and a WebAssembly build — see the repository (MIT or Apache-2.0).
